Off Track

Driver profile

I got into engineering the way a lot of people get into cars: by wanting to know what was happening underneath. Formula 1 is the version of that obsession I never grew out of, because it's a sport where the engineering is the story. A tenth of a second is a design decision someone made months earlier.

That's roughly why I've ended up drawn to hardware systems engineering, test and verification. A race car and a lab bench have the same shape of problem: sensors that lie to you, a system that has to keep working when one subsystem doesn't, and a result nobody should believe until it has been measured. Most of what I've learned on my own builds came from bring-up and debugging, not from the part that worked first try.

Longer term I want to keep going deeper into the hardware itself, through digital logic and computer architecture. Logic Design and Circuit Analysis this term are the first steps on that road.

When I'm not building, I'm usually in Forza Horizon or Forza Motorsport tuning something that doesn't need tuning, out on the bike on a trail, or watching a race.
